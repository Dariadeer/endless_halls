How are spells materialized from the rune it is created in?

These are some of the effect rune concepts that might give the spell its properties. The question is when are we releasing the effects? We could accumulate them, but what if we want channeling? Create rune types that would allow both?

What if we have an accumulation rune that would take the modified mana and add subsequent effects to it until its activation? Another rune could be a channeling rune that would operate in much shorter intervals, allowing to produce near-immediate effects.

Firstly, for effects to take form, they need to be given a physical property and an action. E.g. fire can be shot out as a projectile, cast as an area, applied as a buff or a negative effect. A projectile could go straight ahead, or on a curve. The area could gradually expand or be specific, like a wall. Something more complex could explode in an area to release projectiles flying outwards...

I think we are at play here with at least 2 concepts: expansion and direction. With the former, we get an effect around the target location. With the latter, we can send the effect continuously away from the character. Since we are operation on a common resource, it should be much more expensive to spend it on a large area of impact rather than a single projectile.

Besides, it is wrong to think about it like that in isolation. We have different magic types and equipment. They all should be connected. Think of weapons: a melee weapon like a sword could work well with "directional area" (e.g. a cone/half-circle area of a swing) and some directional capability of a vertical cleave (could be a quickly fading function?). In contrast, something like a bow could be scaled really well for high distances (but struggle with close-quarter spells) and work really bad for area effects (would require a lot of mana). Staffs could be great for channeling specifically, while being equally good with direction and expansion.

How about elemental magic and complex chain effects?

Elemental magic should also impact these values differently, as well as other things like durations. If I had to guess, these would be strength of different elements:
- Fire -- early burst, area, buffs, dot
- Ice -- duration, area, cc, shielding, late burst, obstacles
- Nature -- direction, buffs, cc, healing, obstacles
- Wind -- both area and direction, bursty cc, directional movement
- Light -- area, buffs, burst, healing
- Darkness -- direction, duration, cc, lifesteal
- Arcana -- teleport, time, good accumulation, shielding, utility
- Pure -- same all-rounder (slightly worse on average, a good starter)

This list will probably grow further in the future (hopefully) vertically. If these effects could be combined in a reasonable manner, we could be looking at some interesting spells, but some limits have to be put in place. Some things should not be combinable with each other. For example:  Fire and Ice, Light and Darkness, Nature and Ice/Fire. Is there a place for water? I don't think it would work well in the setting.

The effects could be targeted at parts of the other players' bodies to inflict a different type of damage or CC (or, inflict them in a greater amount if directed to the correct body part). The body parts could be correlated with the equipment slots.
